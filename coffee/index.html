Sustainability / back-of-envelope

Your coffee, in carbon.

Three ordinary things, measured the same way, in grams of CO2-equivalent. Two of them you do every day without thinking. One of them you think about for months. Only one of them matters.

✉︎ One email 0.3g Short, no attachment, written on a laptop.
☕ One latte 550g Dairy milk, conventionally grown beans.
✈ One flight 1.3t London–New York return, economy, one seat.

The part that surprises people

One return flight is about 2,360 lattes — or 4.3 million emails.

That bar is drawn honestly, at a true linear scale — which is why you cannot see the latte. To draw the email you would need a screen about 1.1 kilometres wide. Every chart you have seen comparing these things used a log scale, and a log scale is where the intuition goes to die.

Deleting your inbox is not the lever. Refusing the paper receipt is not the lever.

The lever is the flight, the boiler and the car — and after that, honestly, it is not really an individual lever at all.

None of this means the small things are worthless. Habits are how people rehearse caring about something, and rehearsal matters. It just means we should be honest about which column the number lands in — because a year of guilt about emails is a year not spent on the thing that was a thousand times bigger.

Where the numbers come from

  1. Email — 0.3 g CO2e. Mike Berners-Lee, How Bad Are Bananas? The Carbon Footprint of Everything, 2020 edition. His range runs from 0.03 g for a spam email killed by a filter to 26 g for a long one with a big attachment. 0.3 g is his figure for a short email written on a laptop. Most of that is the electricity your own device burns while you write it, not the sending — which is why the number is soft, and why it moves when people re-estimate it.
  2. Coffee — 550 g, and 330 g grown sustainably. Carmen Nab and Mark Maslin, UCL, Geo: Geography and Environment, 2021. Their per-cup figures: black coffee 280 g, flat white 340 g, cappuccino 410 g, latte 550 g. Under sustainable production those fall to 130 g, 200 g and 330 g. Milk is most of a latte's footprint, so a smaller milky drink is a real reduction, not a rounding error.
  3. Flight — 1.3 t CO2e. Calculated here rather than quoted, so you can check it: London–New York is about 5,555 km each way, so 11,110 km return. UK DESNZ/DEFRA 2025 conversion factors put long-haul economy at 0.117 kg CO₂e per passenger-km, including radiative forcing. 11,110 × 0.117 = 1,300 kg. Strip out radiative forcing and it is nearer 0.9 t; add real routing and holding and it is nearer 1.4 t. There is no single agreed multiplier for the non-CO2 warming effects of contrails, and that disagreement is worth more than the third significant figure.
  4. The average person — 6.6 t CO2e a year. UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2024, global average per-capita greenhouse gas emissions for 2023. Wealthy countries run around three times that.
