# Observation piece — writer's brief

Source: the ESG Tracker dataset as it stands (12 entities, 4 categories), plus the
FY2025 reports read out of Drive. Every figure below was recomputed against the
live data on the date of writing, not recalled.

**Every claim is graded A / B / C.** Grade A you can put in a headline. Grade B
needs the stated caveat travelling with it. Grade C should not be published.

---

## The thesis

**The headline number is the easy part.**

Every entity in this set discloses gross emissions. Almost none discloses the
accounting basis that makes the number mean anything — the consolidation
approach, the Scope 2 method, which Scope 3 categories are in, the GWP vintage.
The metric that gets scored is universally supplied; the qualifiers that make it
interpretable are not.

That is not a story about laggards. It is a story about what disclosure has been
optimised for.

## Claims, with evidence and confidence

### A-grade — verified, publishable as stated

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **One entity of twelve publishes the IFRS S2 ¶29 cross-industry metric set.** Sembcorp discloses (b) transition-risk exposure S$1.0bn/50%, (c) physical-risk exposure S$1.9bn/13%, (d) opportunities S$723M/36%, (e) capital deployment S$626M, (f) internal carbon price S$25–183/tCO₂e. | All eight period-matched reports were searched for these specific terms. Sembcorp is the only one with a table labelled against S2 (29). |
| **Only the three banks dual-report Scope 2.** DBS 50,091 location / 23,571 market; OCBC 78,650 / 30,551; UOB reports on a location basis for its target. Everyone else publishes one basis or none. | Both figures read from each bank's own table. |
| **No entity in the set has reasonable assurance.** Eight of twelve have external assurance; all eight are *limited*. | Assurance provider statements name ISAE 3000/3410 or SSAE 3000/3410 limited engagements throughout. |
| **Twelve entities report across eight different period labels** — FY2024, FY2024/25, FY2025, FY2025/26, FY2025 (ended 30 Jun), FY2025 (ended 31 Dec) and more. | Directly observable in the dataset. |
| **Better measurement looks like worse performance.** OCBC's Scope 1 rose from 132 to 2,001 tCO₂e — because it began counting fugitive emissions and widened entity coverage to align with IFRS. Singtel's Scope 1 rose 57.2% — because it expanded its reporting boundary. Neither is a real increase in emissions. | Both stated in the entities' own reports. |
| **DBS restated every FY2024 emissions figure in its FY2025 report.** Total GHG moved from 83,784 to 81,852 tCO₂e; Scope 1 from 1,300 to 1,484; Scope 2 market-based from 26,322 to 24,871. | Restatement markers on each figure in SR2025. |

### C-grade — do not publish

- **"Only 10% of figures are high decision-usefulness."** That grade requires a
  recorded page number, and we only captured pages for one of four datasets. The
  number reflects our extraction depth as much as their disclosure. Using it as a
  verdict on the companies would be unfair and wrong.

## What this data cannot support

State these limits early in the piece; a reader will otherwise assume you are
claiming them.

- **No rankings.** Most peer comparisons in this set are blocked before you reach
  performance — on reporting period or on organizational boundary. The utilities
  set was never period-comparable at all (Meralco FY2024, CLP FY2025, National
  Grid FY2025/26).
- **No population claims.** Twelve entities, chosen purposively to build a
  comparison tool, not sampled. "In these twelve reports…" is defensible.
  "Singapore-listed companies…" is not.
- **No trends.** One dataset has a multi-year series. Where movement exists it is
  contaminated by boundary changes — see the OCBC and Singtel examples above.
- **No environmental performance claims of any kind.**
- **Four entities are a year stale** (Meralco, DBS, OCBC, UOB). Do not present
  their figures as current.

## Suggested structure

1. **Open on the counterintuitive fact.** OCBC's Scope 1 emissions went up
   fifteen-fold. Nothing changed in the world; they started counting fugitive
   gases. Ask what an investor screening on year-on-year change would have done
   with that.
2. **Widen to the thesis.** Everyone gives you the number. Almost nobody gives
   you what the number means. Four of twelve state their consolidation basis.
   None states its GWP vintage.
3. **Targets.** Seventeen climate targets across twelve companies, and not one
   says whether it intends to reduce emissions or buy them.
4. **Assurance.** Two-thirds have external assurance, and all of it is limited —
   the weaker of the two opinions an auditor can give. Worth explaining the
   difference to a general reader; most won't know there is one.
5. **What this means for comparison.** You cannot rank these companies. Not
   because the data is bad, but because the accounting bases and reporting
   periods do not line up — and nothing in a typical ESG table tells you that.
6. **Close on the asymmetry.** The gap is not effort. It's that the frameworks
   ask for a headline metric and a set of qualifiers, and only the headline
   metric gets scored.

## Fact-check before publishing

- Re-run the figures against the dataset on the day you publish — four entities
  are pending a period refresh and the counts will move.
- Verify IFRS S2 ¶29 and ¶33–37 paragraph references against the current
  standards text. They are recorded here for traceability, not independently
  checked.
- Name entities only where the claim is A-grade. For B-grade counts, describe the
  pattern without attributing a specific failure to a specific company.
- Say plainly that the sample is twelve purposively chosen entities.
