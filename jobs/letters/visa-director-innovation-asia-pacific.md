# Cover note — Director, Innovation, Asia Pacific, Visa

Dear [hiring manager],

I'm writing about the Director, Innovation role for Asia Pacific.

Most people running corporate innovation commission the research and brief the prototype. I run the research, design the concept, and build the prototype myself. That is not aspiration: I currently design, code and deploy AI products end to end using Claude Code and agentic workflows, which puts the distance between a problem and something testable at days rather than weeks. For a role whose output is concept, prototype, validation and handoff, handing over a working artefact instead of a deck changes what the receiving team can do with it.

The method behind that is fifteen years old. As Head of Product and UX at PropertyGuru I ran discovery and design across Singapore, Malaysia, Vietnam and Thailand: core user-journey research and redesign lifted conversion 20% in six months, and the zero-to-one launch of Agent Boost sat on an agent subscription line delivering 25% CAGR that contributed to the Series D valuation increase. I owned both product and UX, so the research was never separable from the commercial decision it existed to serve.

I have also built ventures inside a large corporate rather than only alongside one. At SP Digital I took new digital revenue streams from zero to net profit after tax within four years, and later set up the agile innovation capability that shipped 13 products in 2024 at three-month time-to-value against a 12 to 24-month organisational norm.

On payments: I designed the SP app payment experience across cards, GIRO, PayNow and a points-and-rewards system for 800,000 monthly active users, built an internal e-wallet to MVP, and worked in product and design on billing-on-behalf — the service through which SP bills end customers for competing electricity retailers. That is the biller side rather than the network side; I have not worked in issuing, acquiring or settlement, and I would not claim otherwise.

Where I think I am genuinely ahead rather than catching up is agentic commerce. I build with agents daily, on real products with real users, and I suspect that is rarer among people with fifteen years of corporate product experience than it is among people with three.

What I would be learning is Visa's domain: digital commerce and the payments network itself. I would rather say that plainly than have you find it in week two.

I'd welcome the chance to talk.
